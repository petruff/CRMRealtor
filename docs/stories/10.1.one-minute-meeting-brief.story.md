## Story

As a realtor preparing for a call, showing, listing appointment, or follow-up, I need a concise, evidence-backed client brief so that I can enter the conversation prepared without searching across contacts, notes, tasks, property activity, messages, and transactions.

## User-visible outcome

From Today, a contact record, or a transaction, the user selects **Brief me** and receives one scannable brief containing:

- Relationship context and source.
- Last meaningful interaction.
- Current goal and lifecycle state.
- Confirmed preferences, exclusions, timeline, geography, financing state, and constraints.
- Household, referral, property, appointment, nurture, and transaction context.
- Open commitments owned by the realtor and the client.
- Recent behavior or communication changes.
- One recommended conversation objective.
- Up to three evidence-backed talking points.
- One recommended next action.
- Explicit unknown, stale, contradictory, and unavailable states.
- Direct navigation to the evidence behind every factual section.

The deterministic version remains usable when no generative model is available.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Authorized entry points.** Today, Contact 360, and Transaction routes expose the same server-owned Brief me command through existing application contracts. A client component cannot supply or override workspace authority.
2. **Bounded source set.** The brief may retrieve only records readable by the active membership from canonical repositories: contact, household/relationship, notes, activities, tasks, important dates, pipeline, nurture, property behavior, appointments, transaction/milestones, communication metadata or content allowed by the exact provider scope, and current Omnix memory/recommendation evidence.
3. **Minimum necessary data.** Retrieval requests only the fields needed for the brief. Secrets, OAuth material, raw provider payloads, internal support data, and unrelated contacts never enter the brief or model projection.
4. **Evidence contract.** Every factual brief item contains a safe citation with source type, source record, fact label, source time, as-of time, and safe route. Aggregates identify their deterministic rule and contributing record set.
5. **Truth states.** Each section can render confirmed, estimated, stale, contradictory, unknown, unavailable, or omitted. Missing facts are never completed from model memory.
6. **Deterministic brief.** A model-free brief assembles the most recent relationship, work, property, and transaction facts; identifies open commitments and current next action; and produces a usable conversation objective from versioned rules.
7. **Optional narrative.** When an approved model is available, Omnix may compress the deterministic brief into a natural narrative and talking points. The narrative cannot introduce a fact not present in the structured evidence set.
8. **Prompt security.** Contact text, notes, messages, transcripts, imports, property descriptions, and user text remain untrusted content. Existing Omnix guard, projection, model-policy, budget, timeout, citation, and fallback controls apply.
9. **Snapshot and invalidation.** A generated MeetingBriefSnapshot stores source hashes, as-of time, policy/rule version, model state, and expiry. A relevant source change marks the snapshot stale; refresh creates a new version without deleting earlier evidence.
10. **One-screen hierarchy.** The first viewport answers: who this is, why the meeting matters, what changed, open commitments, and the recommended objective. Supporting details and evidence use progressive disclosure.
11. **No unsupported prediction.** Suggested questions, objections, and conversation guidance are labeled as suggestions. The brief does not state protected-class, legal, financial, appraisal, lending, insurance, tax, school-quality, safety, or investment conclusions.
12. **Performance.** The deterministic shell should render promptly from bounded queries. Model narrative streams or resolves independently and cannot block source facts, navigation, or next-action controls.
13. **Accessibility.** Desktop and mobile interfaces preserve semantic headings, keyboard flow, visible focus, screen-reader state changes, 44-pixel touch targets, 200-percent zoom, reduced motion, and no document overflow.
14. **Observability.** Each generation records redacted correlation, actor, workspace, source counts, rule/policy/model versions, freshness, latency, token/cost, guard result, fallback, and terminal state without storing hidden prompts or unauthorized content.
15. **Evaluation.** Fixtures cover missing facts, conflicting preferences, changed timelines, negation, multiple commitments, ambiguous relationship roles, stale property events, inaccessible provider content, prompt injection, cross-workspace IDs, budget exhaustion, timeout, and model failure.
16. **Release evidence.** Lint, TypeScript, focused tests, full tests, build, migration/RLS where added, authenticated owner/assistant UAT, and 390/768/1440 browser evidence pass on the exact candidate.

## Interaction design

### Today

An appointment or priority card exposes Brief me. The initial card shows:

- Contact and meeting purpose.
- Meeting time and channel.
- One-line preparation reason.
- Brief freshness.
- Primary action: Brief me.
- Secondary action: open contact or transaction.

### Brief view

Order:

1. **Conversation objective**
2. **What changed**
3. **Relationship at a glance**
4. **Open commitments**
5. **Preferences and constraints**
6. **Property and transaction context**
7. **Suggested talking points**
8. **Recommended next action**
9. **Evidence and generation details**

The interface must avoid a chat transcript for this job. It is a decision card designed for scanning before a conversation.

### Failure and degraded states

- No model: deterministic brief is available.
- No meaningful history: show the exact missing context and propose safe discovery questions.
- Stale snapshot: show source change and refresh action.
- Contradictory facts: show both sources and request confirmation.
- Provider disconnected: use stored CRM facts and name the missing channel.
- Unauthorized source: omit it without leaking existence.
- Generation failure: preserve deterministic facts and a retry action.
- Budget limit: preserve deterministic facts and state when model narration can be retried.

## Domain contract

Logical type:

- MeetingBriefSnapshot
  - id
  - workspaceId
  - subjectContactId
  - optionalTransactionId
  - optionalAppointmentId
  - trigger
  - asOf
  - expiresAt
  - status
  - sourceHashes
  - deterministicRuleVersion
  - optionalModelPolicyVersion
  - optionalModelRoute
  - structuredBrief
  - citations
  - generationState
  - createdByMembershipId
  - createdAt

A physical schema is optional for the first slice if source-hash caching and auditability can be satisfied through the existing proposal or memory authority. Architecture must make that decision before implementation.

## Application contract

Recommended commands:

- buildMeetingBrief(scope, input, clock)
- refreshMeetingBrief(scope, snapshotId, expectedVersion, clock)
- getMeetingBrief(scope, snapshotId)
- listMeetingBriefEvidence(scope, snapshotId)

Input IDs are resolved through workspace-scoped repositories. The output uses a versioned machine-readable envelope shared by CLI and UI.

## Success measures

Measure baseline before targets:

- Brief-open rate before appointments.
- Median time from Brief me to usable deterministic content.
- Model narrative completion and fallback rates.
- Evidence drill-through rate.
- User correction rate.
- Percentage of briefs with one valid next action.
- Follow-up completion after brief use.
- Repeat use in the next seven days.

## Non-goals

- Silent contact, task, pipeline, nurture, transaction, or provider mutation.
- Hidden personality, protected-class, or emotional-state profiling.
- Legal, financial, lending, appraisal, insurance, tax, safety, school-quality, or investment advice.
- Replacing the evidence timeline with generated prose.
- Blocking the meeting brief when a model or provider is unavailable.
- Claiming production readiness from sample or local data.

## Definition of done

The story is done only when a representative owner and authorized assistant can open the same current, cited brief from Today and Contact 360 on desktop and mobile; deterministic fallback works; source changes mark the brief stale; inaccessible data does not leak; all quality gates pass; and the exact release state is documented without implying that a provider or model is operational unless real evidence proves it.
