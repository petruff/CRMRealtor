## Story

**As Judith or her authorized assistant,**

**I want** one persistent, prioritized attention queue that Omnix, Today and Alerts share,

**so that** I can acknowledge, defer and complete the right work in priority order without the same alert reappearing as an anonymous read-only record.

**Traceability:** ROS-FR-01, ROS-FR-05, ROS-FR-12, ROS-FR-15; ROS-NFR-01–04, ROS-NFR-08–10; Stories 3.1, 3.6, 3.12, 3.14 and 3.27; `docs/architecture/omnix-operations-white-label-architecture.md`.

**Commercial boundary:** The approved white-label monthly/annual direction is recorded in Epic 6 and the architecture decision. This story introduces no billing provider, checkout, plan, price, trial, subscription enforcement or billing UI. Judith remains a free early-access user and sees no billing surface.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. **CLI-first operational contract.** A documented non-interactive CLI can reconcile authorized canonical alerts into durable attention occurrences, list the ordered queue and perform acknowledge, snooze, complete, dismiss where allowed, assign and reopen transitions. The CLI uses the same domain, application and repository contracts as the UI, returns stable JSON and non-zero failure codes, requires authenticated live workspace authority, and does not print contact PII unless explicit detail is requested.
2. **Stable occurrence identity.** Each attention item persists canonical `workspace_id`, rule/category, subject type/ID, stable occurrence key, source fingerprint, reason, safe internal href, priority class, SLA/due instant when known, immutable enqueue sequence, optional assignee, state, lifecycle timestamps and sanitized evidence. Relative wording and the current date do not define occurrence identity. A composite workspace relationship prevents cross-tenant subjects and assignees.
3. **Deterministic priority with FIFO.** Active items sort by effective class P0–P4, SLA breach, due/SLA instant, immutable enqueue sequence and stable ID. FIFO applies only inside an equivalent class/deadline. Explicit bounded aging may promote old P2–P4 items by at most one class; it cannot outrank a current P0 inbound response, transaction deadline or critical compliance/connector failure. The same repository snapshot produces the same order in CLI, Omnix, Today and Alerts.
4. **Auditable lifecycle.** Allowed states are `open`, `acknowledged`, `snoozed`, `completed`, `dismissed` and `escalated`. Snooze requires a future instant; dismiss requires an allowlisted rule and bounded reason; terminal transitions record actor/time/reason; system reconciliation records system actor and reason. Every transition is idempotent, optimistic-concurrency protected and appends immutable lifecycle evidence. Invalid, stale-version, cross-workspace and disallowed transitions fail closed without partial mutation.
5. **Truthful reconciliation.** Reconciliation materializes only alerts produced by the authorized deterministic Omnix catalog. Replaying the same source fingerprint is a no-op. A completed/dismissed occurrence is not recreated while the authoritative fingerprint is unchanged. A materially changed source fact creates or reopens the appropriate occurrence exactly once. When a source condition clears, reconciliation records a system resolution without fabricating a call, email, text, appointment or provider success.
6. **Bounded scheduler and recovery.** A server-only scheduled route runs the same reconciliation service under configured automation workspace authority. It requires constant-time cron authorization, leases bounded work, records run/attempt/result counters, retries transient failures with bounded backoff and moves exhausted or ambiguous work to review. The worker never accepts caller-supplied workspace authority, free-form executable instructions or browser credentials. Stopping the scheduler leaves existing queue data readable and actionable.
7. **One operating projection.** Omnix “Who needs attention?”, the Today command center and `/alerts` consume the same ordered attention projection. Every visible item leads with the person/task/system issue, plain-language reason, priority/SLA explanation, last known context and safe action. The UI provides acknowledge, snooze and complete controls where authorized; destructive or provider-facing actions remain separate approval-gated commands.
8. **Preferences boundary.** This story may persist workspace timezone and minimal in-app queue preferences needed for deterministic evaluation, but it does not claim email, SMS or push notification delivery. Quiet hours, notification channels and provider delivery receipts remain the next bounded story unless already backed by real provider evidence.
9. **Workspace and role isolation.** Owner and active assistant may read and work assigned/visible queue items according to typed product permissions; owner-only policy changes and disallowed dismissals remain protected. Revoked members, another workspace, browser-supplied IDs and service-role shortcuts cannot read or mutate the queue. Two-workspace and owner/assistant negative tests cover tables, RPCs, repositories, routes and commands.
10. **Judith continuity and white-label compatibility.** Existing Judith contacts, tasks, alerts, provider connections and current Omnix brand remain backward compatible. No billing navigation, paywall, trial countdown, invoice, price or payment request is rendered. Every new persisted record is workspace-scoped so later brand profiles and entitlements can be added without a data migration that changes tenant authority.
11. **Accessible premium workflow.** The shared queue works at 390, 768 and 1440 pixels with keyboard operation, visible focus, 44px touch targets, screen-reader status announcements, reduced-motion support, no horizontal overflow and progressive disclosure for large queues. Loading, empty, partial, stale-version, scheduler-unavailable and transition-failure states explain what changed and what did not.
12. **Quality and release evidence.** Domain, repository, RLS, CLI, application, component and route tests prove identity, replay, ordering, aging, lifecycle, concurrency, reconciliation, role isolation and Judith no-billing behavior. Migration replay and documented forward-repair/rollback are validated. `npm run lint`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm test` and `npm run build` pass from `packages/crm`; @qa records an independent verdict before any release handoff.

## Out of Scope

- Billing provider selection, plans, prices, trials, checkout, invoices, taxes, refunds or paid entitlement enforcement.
- Workspace brand editor, custom domain activation or customer-facing white-label onboarding.
- Autonomous external email, SMS, Mailchimp campaign, pipeline mutation, merge, deletion or financial action.
- Generative ranking or model-created urgency; models may summarize only the deterministic queue in later governed scope.
- Property, transaction, commission and P&L domain creation.
- Claims that email, SMS or push notifications were delivered without provider receipts.

### Architecture and existing seams

- The canonical distinction between attention items and explicit human tasks, lifecycle, ordering, reconciliation, white-label boundary and Judith early-access rule is defined in `docs/architecture/omnix-operations-white-label-architecture.md`. [Source: `docs/architecture/omnix-operations-white-label-architecture.md`]
- Existing task persistence, immutable activities, CLI conventions, workspace RLS and bounded work-queue behavior must be extended rather than replaced. [Source: `docs/stories/3.1.crm-work-queue-foundation.story.md`]
- Current Omnix alerts are a deterministic read-only projection and preserve fixed ordering/citations; this story adds persistence and operations without allowing a model to rescore them. [Source: `docs/stories/3.6.deterministic-organizational-copilot.story.md`; `docs/stories/3.27.omnix-people-first-attention-brief.story.md`]

- Epic 6 already requires persistent occurrence identity, acknowledgement, snooze, resolution, preferences, timezone, quiet hours and auditable delivery, but delivery channels are deliberately split from this first lifecycle increment. [Source: `docs/prd/epic-6-independent-realtor-operating-system.md#wave-1--activation-and-notification-operations`]
- New scheduler work must reuse the established server-only cron authorization and automation workspace authority patterns. [Source: `packages/crm/lib/security/connector-cron-auth.ts`; `packages/crm/lib/data/automation-context.ts`; `packages/crm/app/api/internal/connectors/drain/route.ts`]

### Security and failure constraints

- The assistant receives complete authorized read visibility through application services, never direct service-role/database authority.
- Evidence is allowlisted and sanitized; no raw provider body, token, secret, header or free-form executable automation is persisted.
- Reconciliation and transitions use stable idempotency keys and optimistic versions; retries cannot duplicate occurrences or events.
- Scheduler failure is observable and recoverable and does not make the interactive queue unavailable.
- Existing unrelated work, including `.aexos/project-status.yaml`, remains preserved.

## Testing

- **Pure domain:** identity/fingerprint stability; every state transition; disallowed dismiss; future snooze; priority/FIFO/aging; stable ties.
- **Repository/SQL:** memory/Supabase parity; composite tenant links; forced RLS; replay/no-op; optimistic conflict; append-only events; scheduler lease/retry/review.
- **Application:** canonical alert reconciliation; cleared/changed facts; no fabricated activity; shared projection parity across surfaces.
- **CLI:** every command, JSON schema, exit codes, explicit-detail PII boundary, authenticated live mode and no silent sample fallback.
- **UI:** person-first actions, progressive disclosure, stale/error recovery, keyboard, reduced motion and 390/768/1440 layouts.
- **Commercial regression:** no billing UI or payment requirement in Judith/current early-access behavior.
